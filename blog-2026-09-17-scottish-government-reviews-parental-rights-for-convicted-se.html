Scottish Government Reviews Parental Rights for Convicted Sex Offenders

17 September 2026

In a significant move to bolster child protection, the Scottish Government has announced a review into the parental rights of convicted sex offenders. This initiative seeks to address concerns that individuals with such convictions can retain parental responsibilities, potentially compromising children's safety.

Under current Scottish law, a criminal conviction does not automatically strip an individual of their Parental Rights and Responsibilities (PRRs). This means that even after being convicted of serious offences, individuals may still have legal rights concerning their children. Protective parents often find themselves navigating complex and emotionally taxing civil court proceedings to limit or remove the offender's access to their children.

The forthcoming review aims to explore the possibility of automatically restricting or suspending PRRs for those convicted of sexual offences. Such a change would alleviate the burden on protective parents and place the onus on the convicted individual to demonstrate their suitability for parental involvement.

For families affected by this issue, the review offers a glimmer of hope. It signifies a potential shift towards a legal framework that prioritises children's safety and well-being, ensuring that parental rights are granted only when they align with the best interests of the child.
