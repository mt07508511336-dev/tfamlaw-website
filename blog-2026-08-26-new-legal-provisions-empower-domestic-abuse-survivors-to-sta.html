New Legal Provisions Empower Domestic Abuse Survivors to Stay in Their Homes

26 August 2026

As of 1 August 2026, significant legal changes have come into effect in Scotland, offering enhanced support to survivors of domestic abuse. These provisions empower social landlords to take decisive action against perpetrators, facilitating a safer environment for victims within their own homes.

Under the new legislation, social landlords can now apply for a court order to end the tenancy of an abuser. If granted, this order allows the landlord to transfer the tenancy directly to the survivor, enabling them to remain in, or return to, their home without the need to relocate. This shift places the responsibility on landlords, rather than survivors, to initiate the removal of the abuser from the tenancy.

Equalities Minister Simita Kumar emphasized the importance of these changes, stating: "Domestic abuse must be eradicated from our society and I am determined to protect the housing rights of women and children, working with the sector to find victims the safest and most suitable form of accommodation to begin to rebuild their lives."

At Thompson Family Law, we recognize the profound impact that stable housing has on the recovery and well-being of domestic abuse survivors. These legal advancements provide a crucial tool for ensuring that victims can maintain their living situations without the added stress of seeking alternative accommodation.

For those affected by domestic abuse, understanding your rights is essential. If you are a tenant in social housing and experiencing domestic abuse, it's important to know that your landlord now has the authority to act on your behalf to remove the abuser from the tenancy. This process aims to prioritize your safety and housing stability.

We encourage anyone facing such circumstances to reach out for support. Our team at Thompson Family Law is here to provide guidance and assistance, ensuring that you are informed of your rights and the options available to you under the new legal framework.

These legislative changes mark a significant step forward in supporting domestic abuse survivors in Scotland. By enabling social landlords to take proactive measures, the law aims to create a safer and more supportive environment for those in need.
