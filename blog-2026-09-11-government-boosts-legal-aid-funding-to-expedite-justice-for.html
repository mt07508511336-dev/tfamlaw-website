Government Boosts Legal Aid Funding to Expedite Justice for Victims

11 September 2026

In a significant move to enhance the efficiency of the justice system, the UK government has announced an investment of up to £34 million annually into criminal legal aid. This funding aims to strengthen the legal sector, facilitate the progression of more cases through the courts, and ultimately reduce delays for victims.

Under the new proposals, barristers undertaking legal aid work will receive an average fee increase of 11%. This adjustment acknowledges their crucial role in delivering justice and supports efforts to address court backlogs. Additionally, the government is considering higher fees for barristers handling cases where defendants enter guilty pleas and for plea and trial preparation hearings. These changes aim to better reflect the complexity and preparation these cases require, while also supporting junior barristers and fostering a sustainable pipeline of legal talent.

With over 80,000 cases currently pending in the Crown Court, this investment is a vital step toward restoring capacity and ensuring that victims receive timely justice. The initiative is part of a broader plan to build a stronger, more sustainable justice system.
