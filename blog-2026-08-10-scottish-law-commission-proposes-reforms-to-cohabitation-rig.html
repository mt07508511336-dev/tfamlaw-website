Scottish Law Commission Proposes Reforms to Cohabitation Rights

10 August 2026

In recent developments, the Scottish Law Commission has put forward proposals aimed at reforming the legal rights of cohabiting couples. With a significant number of couples in Scotland choosing to live together without formalizing their relationship through marriage or civil partnership, these reforms seek to address the legal challenges that can arise when such relationships end.

Currently, cohabiting couples have limited legal protections compared to their married counterparts. The existing laws, primarily established by the Family Law (Scotland) Act 2006, have been criticized for being outdated and unclear. The Commission's discussion paper highlights several key areas for potential reform:

  • Definition of 'Cohabitant': Clarifying who qualifies as a cohabitant to ensure consistent legal application.
  • Financial Provision on Relationship Breakdown: Evaluating whether cohabitants should have similar rights to financial support as married couples upon separation.
  • Minimum Cohabitation Period: Considering if a minimum duration of cohabitation should be required before legal rights are conferred.

These proposed changes aim to provide clearer guidelines and better protection for individuals in cohabiting relationships, reflecting the evolving nature of family structures in Scotland.
