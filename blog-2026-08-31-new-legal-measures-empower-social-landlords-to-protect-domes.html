New Legal Measures Empower Social Landlords to Protect Domestic Abuse Victims

31 August 2026

As of 1 August 2026, significant legal changes have come into effect in Scotland, empowering social landlords to better support tenants experiencing domestic abuse. These amendments provide a new discretionary ground for eviction, enabling landlords to take decisive action against perpetrators and offer greater protection to victims.

Under the updated legislation, social landlords can now apply to the court to end an abuser's tenancy and transfer it to the victim. This means that, where appropriate, victims can remain in their homes without the need to initiate legal proceedings themselves. The court will assess whether all conditions are met before granting such an order, ensuring a fair process for all parties involved.

These changes aim to remove barriers that often prevent victims from leaving abusive situations. By allowing landlords to intervene directly, the burden on victims to seek legal remedies is reduced, providing a more straightforward path to safety and stability.

It's important to note that while these provisions offer new avenues for support, they are discretionary. Social landlords are encouraged to work closely with domestic abuse services and the victims themselves to determine the most appropriate course of action. This collaborative approach ensures that decisions are made in the best interest of the victim and their household.

For those affected by domestic abuse, understanding these new rights is crucial. If you're a tenant in a social housing property and are experiencing domestic abuse, reach out to your landlord to discuss the support available under these new provisions. Additionally, organisations like Scottish Women's Aid offer resources and guidance to help navigate these challenging situations.

At Thompson Family Law, we recognise the profound impact domestic abuse has on individuals and families. These legal developments represent a positive step towards providing victims with the support and protection they need.
