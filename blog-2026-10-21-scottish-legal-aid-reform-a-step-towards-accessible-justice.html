Scottish Legal Aid Reform: A Step Towards Accessible Justice

21 October 2026

Access to justice is a cornerstone of a fair society. Recognising this, the Scottish Government has initiated a consultation aimed at reforming the legal aid system. This move seeks to address longstanding concerns about the system's effectiveness and accessibility.

Legal aid provides financial assistance to individuals who might otherwise be unable to afford legal representation. However, in recent years, challenges have emerged. Reports indicate a significant decline in solicitors willing to take on legal aid cases, leading to 'legal aid deserts' in parts of Scotland. This shortage has forced many individuals to represent themselves in court, often to their detriment. For instance, a 2024 survey by Shared Parenting Scotland revealed that 20% of respondents eligible for legal aid had to represent themselves due to the unavailability of solicitors willing to take on their cases. ([scottishlegal.com](https://www.scottishlegal.com/articles/surge-in-parents-representing-themselves-in-scottish-family-courts?utm_source=openai))

The current consultation aims to modernise the legal aid system, making it more responsive to the needs of today's society. Key proposals include simplifying the application process, ensuring fair remuneration for solicitors, and expanding the scope of services covered under legal aid. The Scottish Legal Aid Board (SLAB) has emphasised the need for 'transformational change' to address the existing shortcomings. ([scottishlegal.com](https://www.scottishlegal.com/category/news/legal-aid?utm_source=openai))

For individuals and families navigating legal challenges, these reforms could be transformative. A more efficient and accessible legal aid system means that those facing family disputes, civil issues, or other legal matters can secure the representation they need without undue financial strain.
