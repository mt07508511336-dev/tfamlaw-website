Scottish Government Seeks Public Input on Legal Aid Reform

29 August 2026

The Scottish Government has recently launched a consultation to gather public opinions on proposed reforms to the legal aid system. This initiative aims to modernize the framework, making it more efficient and accessible for those who rely on legal aid services.

Legal aid is a vital service that provides publicly funded legal assistance to individuals who cannot afford legal representation. It covers various areas, including family law, housing, debt, and criminal law. The current system, governed by the Legal Aid (Scotland) Act 1986, supports approximately 170,000 grants each year, with expenditures reaching £169 million in 2024-25—the highest annual spend to date.

The proposed reforms focus on several key areas:

  • Simplifying Processes: Streamlining procedures to reduce duplication and support quicker decision-making.
  • Improving Access: Enhancing the system to be more user-friendly for those seeking legal aid.
  • Empowering Solicitors: Providing greater autonomy for accredited solicitors to deliver services effectively.

These changes are part of a broader effort to create a legal aid system that is fairer, more accessible, and fit for the future. Minister for Victims and Community Safety, Kirsten Oswald, emphasized the importance of this initiative, stating, "Together, we can build a legal aid system that is fairer, more accessible and fit for the future."

In addition to these reforms, a 13% increase in solicitor fees is set to take effect in September. This adjustment aims to address concerns within the legal profession about the sustainability of providing legal aid services. Previous surveys have indicated that a significant number of solicitors were considering leaving legal aid work due to financial pressures.

The consultation is open until 13 October 2026, and the Scottish Government encourages all interested parties to share their views. This is a crucial opportunity for individuals and professionals to contribute to shaping a legal aid system that meets the needs of Scotland's citizens.
