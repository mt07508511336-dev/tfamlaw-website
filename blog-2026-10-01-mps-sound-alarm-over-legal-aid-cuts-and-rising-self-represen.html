MPs Sound Alarm Over Legal Aid Cuts and Rising Self-Representation

1 October 2026

Recent findings from the UK Parliament's Justice Committee have raised serious concerns about the state of legal aid in England and Wales. The committee's report highlights that the diminishing availability of legal aid, coupled with a rise in individuals representing themselves in court, poses an "unacceptably high risk of miscarriages of justice" and undermines the efficient functioning of the legal system.

Legal aid is designed to ensure that everyone, regardless of their financial situation, has access to legal representation. However, the report points out that eligibility criteria for both criminal and civil legal aid have not kept pace with the rising cost of living. This has led to a significant "justice gap," where many individuals find themselves unable to afford legal representation but also ineligible for legal aid.

One particularly troubling aspect is the increase in self-representation. Without proper legal training, individuals may struggle to navigate the complexities of the legal system, potentially leading to unjust outcomes. The committee emphasizes that this trend not only jeopardizes individual cases but also places additional strain on the court system, as proceedings can become prolonged and less efficient.

To address these issues, the committee recommends comprehensive reforms. These include revising eligibility thresholds to reflect current economic realities, ensuring fair remuneration for legal aid providers to maintain a sustainable service, and simplifying administrative processes to make legal aid more accessible.
