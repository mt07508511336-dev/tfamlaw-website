Scottish Ministers Implement Key Provisions of Children's Care and Justice Act

16 November 2026

Starting December 1, 2026, Scottish Ministers will enforce six provisions of the Children (Care and Justice) (Scotland) Act 2024. This move signifies a substantial advancement in Scotland's commitment to child protection and justice reform.

The provisions being implemented encompass several critical areas:

  • Trauma-Informed Practices: Introducing duties to ensure that children's hearings are conducted with an understanding of trauma, aiming to create a supportive environment for vulnerable children.
  • Movement Restriction Conditions: Establishing guidelines for imposing movement restrictions on children, balancing the need for safety with the child's rights and well-being.
  • Secure Accommodation: Setting clear criteria and procedures for placing children in secure accommodation, ensuring such measures are used appropriately and judiciously.

These changes are part of a broader effort to align Scotland's child protection framework with contemporary best practices. By focusing on trauma-informed approaches, the legislation acknowledges the profound impact that adverse experiences can have on children and seeks to mitigate further harm within the justice system.

For families navigating these changes, it's essential to understand how these provisions might affect them. The emphasis on trauma-informed hearings means that children's voices and experiences will be central to proceedings, potentially leading to more empathetic and effective outcomes. Additionally, the clear guidelines on movement restrictions and secure accommodation aim to ensure that such interventions are used appropriately, safeguarding children's rights.
