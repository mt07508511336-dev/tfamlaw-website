New Powers for Social Landlords to Protect Domestic Abuse Victims

20 October 2026

At Thompson Family Law, we are committed to keeping our clients informed about significant legal developments that impact families across Scotland. A recent legislative change aims to provide stronger protections for victims of domestic abuse, particularly concerning their housing rights.

Effective from 1 August 2026, social landlords in Scotland will have the authority to transfer a tenancy from an abusive partner to the victim. This change is part of the Domestic Abuse (Protection) (Scotland) Act 2021, specifically under Part 2 of the Act. Previously, victims often faced the distressing choice of staying in an unsafe environment or leaving their homes to escape abuse. This new provision empowers social landlords to take legal action to end the tenancy of the abuser, allowing the victim to remain in their home safely.

Housing Secretary Màiri McAllan emphasized the importance of this measure, stating that domestic abuse must be eradicated from our society and that protecting the housing rights of women and children is a priority. By enabling social landlords to intervene directly, the legislation aims to reduce the upheaval victims face during such challenging times.

For individuals experiencing domestic abuse, this development offers a pathway to maintain stability and security in their living situations. If you or someone you know is affected by domestic abuse and has concerns about housing, it's crucial to seek advice promptly. Our team at Thompson Family Law is here to provide compassionate and practical guidance tailored to your circumstances.

Understanding your rights and the support available can make a significant difference. We encourage anyone in need to reach out to us or other support services to explore the options available under this new legal framework.
