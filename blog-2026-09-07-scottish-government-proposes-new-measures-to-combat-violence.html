Scottish Government Proposes New Measures to Combat Violence Against Women

7 September 2026

In a significant move to address the persistent issue of violence against women and girls, the Scottish Government has unveiled a series of initiatives aimed at fostering a safer environment for all. First Minister John Swinney announced plans to introduce a misogyny bill and launch a public health campaign targeting misogynistic attitudes among boys and young men. These measures come in response to alarming statistics indicating a rise in sexual crimes across Scotland.

Recent data reveals that the number of reported rapes and attempted rapes has increased by nearly one-third over the past four years, reaching 3,240 cases in the year leading up to June 2026. Overall, sexual crimes have risen by 11% during the same period, with a 9% uptick in the last 12 months alone. These figures underscore the urgent need for comprehensive strategies to combat such offenses.

The proposed misogyny bill aims to criminalize behaviors that perpetuate hatred and discrimination against women, sending a clear message that such conduct will not be tolerated. Complementing this legislative effort, the public health campaign seeks to challenge and change harmful attitudes by engaging with young males, promoting respect and equality from an early age.

While these initiatives have been welcomed by many, some advocates believe they may not go far enough. Liz Shanks, a survivor of domestic abuse, expressed concerns that the proposals might be perceived as a "tick-box exercise" and called for more substantial reforms within the justice system to protect victims effectively.
