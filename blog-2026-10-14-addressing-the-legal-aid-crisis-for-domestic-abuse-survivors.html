Addressing the Legal Aid Crisis for Domestic Abuse Survivors in Scotland

14 October 2026

Recent reports have highlighted a pressing issue in Scotland: a significant shortage of legal aid is making it increasingly difficult for women to escape abusive relationships. This shortage is reaching crisis levels, leaving many vulnerable individuals without the necessary legal support to navigate complex civil proceedings.

Legal aid is a government-funded program designed to ensure that individuals who cannot afford legal representation can still access justice. For survivors of domestic abuse, this support is crucial. It enables them to pursue protective orders, resolve child custody disputes, and address financial matters arising from separation. Without legal aid, many are forced to face the courts alone, a daunting prospect that can deter them from seeking the protection and justice they deserve.

One of the primary challenges is the financial strain on legal aid solicitors. The current funding model has made it increasingly unsustainable for lawyers to take on legal aid cases. This has led to a decline in the number of solicitors willing or able to offer such services, thereby reducing access for those in need.

Moreover, the process of applying for legal aid can be retraumatizing for survivors. It often requires them to recount their experiences in detail, which can be emotionally exhausting and may deter them from proceeding. Additionally, prolonged court proceedings can be exploited by abusers to maintain control over their victims, further emphasizing the need for timely and accessible legal support.

Addressing this crisis requires a multifaceted approach. Firstly, there is a need for a comprehensive review and reform of the legal aid funding model to ensure that providing such services is financially viable for solicitors. This could involve increasing the rates paid for legal aid work or offering additional support to firms that take on these cases.

Secondly, simplifying the application process for legal aid can make it more accessible. Streamlining paperwork and providing support services to assist applicants can alleviate some of the emotional burdens associated with the process.

Lastly, increasing awareness and training among legal professionals about the dynamics of domestic abuse can lead to more sensitive handling of cases, ensuring that survivors feel supported throughout the legal process.
