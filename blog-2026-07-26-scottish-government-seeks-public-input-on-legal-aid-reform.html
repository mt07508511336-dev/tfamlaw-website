Scottish Government Seeks Public Input on Legal Aid Reform

26 July 2026

The Scottish Government has recently launched a consultation to gather public input on proposed reforms to the legal aid system. This initiative aims to modernise the framework, making it more efficient and accessible for both those who rely on legal aid and the professionals who provide it.

Legal aid is a vital service that offers publicly funded legal assistance to individuals who might otherwise be unable to afford it. It covers various legal matters, including family law issues such as divorce, child custody, and civil disputes. The current system, governed by the Legal Aid (Scotland) Act 1986, has been instrumental in ensuring access to justice for many. However, over the years, it has become evident that updates are necessary to address emerging challenges and improve service delivery.

The consultation outlines several key proposals:

  • Simplifying Processes: Streamlining procedures to reduce duplication and expedite decision-making, ensuring quicker access to legal aid for those in need.
  • Enhancing Access: Improving the system to make it more user-friendly, ensuring that individuals can easily navigate and benefit from legal aid services.
  • Empowering Solicitors: Providing greater autonomy to accredited solicitors, allowing them to make decisions that best serve their clients' interests.

These reforms are part of a broader effort to align the legal aid system with the Scottish Government's Public Service Reform agenda, focusing on efficiency, value for money, and improved outcomes through more streamlined services.

Minister for Victims and Community Safety, Kirsten Oswald, emphasized the importance of this initiative, stating, "At its heart, this is about building a legal aid system that works better for the people who rely on it and the professionals who deliver it." She encourages all stakeholders, including the public and legal professionals, to participate in the consultation process to help shape a fairer and more accessible legal aid system.

The consultation is open until 13 October 2026, and individuals can submit their views online. This is a valuable opportunity for the public to influence the future of legal aid in Scotland, ensuring it meets the needs of all citizens.
