Scottish Court Grants Appeal in Council Tenant's Eviction Case

3 November 2026

In a recent development, the Sheriff Appeal Court has granted a South Lanarkshire Council tenant the right to appeal her eviction case to the Court of Session. This case brings to the forefront important questions about how eviction procedures intersect with tenants' human rights.

The tenant, Ms. Elaine McConnell, has resided in her council property since 1996. In March 2024, her adult son, who lived with her, was convicted of a drug-related offence. Notably, Ms. McConnell was neither present during the offence nor aware of it. Following this, the council initiated eviction proceedings using the streamlined process outlined in the Housing (Scotland) Act 2001.

Initially, a summary sheriff declined the eviction application, considering factors such as Ms. McConnell's health, her longstanding tenancy without prior incidents, and the positive impact of her son's community-based sentence. However, the Sheriff Appeal Court has now certified the case for appeal, indicating that it raises unresolved questions about the application of Article 8 of the European Convention on Human Rights in such eviction proceedings.

This case underscores the delicate balance between a landlord's right to reclaim property and a tenant's right to respect for their home and family life. It highlights the necessity for eviction processes to be fair and proportionate, especially when tenants are not directly involved in the actions leading to eviction.

For tenants facing similar situations, this development emphasizes the importance of seeking legal advice to understand their rights fully. At Thompson Family Law, we are committed to providing clear, compassionate guidance to navigate these complex legal landscapes.

As this case progresses, it may set a precedent for how human rights considerations are integrated into eviction proceedings in Scotland. We will continue to monitor and provide updates on this significant legal matter.
