Scottish Court Recognises Link Between Domestic Abuse and Suicide in Landmark Ruling

1 September 2026

In a groundbreaking decision earlier this year, the Scottish legal system has, for the first time, held a domestic abuser criminally responsible for his spouse's suicide. This landmark case underscores the profound impact that sustained abuse can have on victims, leading to tragic outcomes.

Lee Milne, a 40-year-old from Dundee, was convicted of culpable homicide following the death of his wife, Kimberly, in July 2023. The court found that Milne's persistent abusive behaviour was a significant factor leading to Kimberly's decision to take her own life. Consequently, he was sentenced to eight years in prison. This ruling is a pivotal moment in Scottish law, as it acknowledges the direct link between domestic abuse and the mental health deterioration of victims, potentially culminating in suicide.

Historically, the legal system has treated domestic abuse and suicide as separate issues. However, this case sets a precedent by recognising that the psychological torment inflicted by an abuser can drive a victim to such despair that they see no other escape. This recognition is vital for several reasons:

  • Legal Accountability: Perpetrators can now be held accountable not just for the immediate physical or emotional harm they cause but also for the long-term consequences of their actions, including the ultimate tragedy of a victim's suicide.
  • Victim Support: This ruling highlights the necessity for comprehensive support systems for victims, addressing both their immediate safety and long-term mental health needs.
  • Public Awareness: By bringing this issue to the forefront, the case raises public awareness about the severe and lasting impacts of domestic abuse, encouraging society to take a more proactive stance against it.

For those experiencing domestic abuse, it's crucial to know that support is available. Organisations such as Women's Aid and Refuge offer confidential assistance and can help individuals navigate their options. Additionally, legal aid is accessible to those who need it, ensuring that financial constraints do not prevent victims from seeking justice and protection.
