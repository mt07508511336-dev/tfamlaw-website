Scottish Government to Review Parental Rights of Convicted Offenders

14 September 2026

In a significant development for family law in Scotland, the Scottish Government has announced a review of the legal framework governing parental rights and responsibilities (PRRs) for individuals convicted of serious offences, including sexual crimes. This initiative responds to growing concerns about the potential risks posed to children when convicted offenders retain automatic parental rights.

Under current Scottish law, a criminal conviction does not automatically strip an individual of their PRRs. This means that even those found guilty of serious crimes can maintain or seek contact with their children, often leading to protracted and distressing legal battles for the non-offending parent. The forthcoming review aims to explore options for automatically restricting or suspending PRRs in cases involving certain serious convictions, thereby prioritising the safety and well-being of children.

Victims Minister Kirsten Oswald confirmed the government's commitment to this review, stating that they will "undertake a review of the current legal position in Scotland specifically to understand the options that are available." This move has been welcomed by survivors and advocacy groups who have long campaigned for reforms to better protect children from potential harm.

For families navigating these complex situations, this review offers a glimmer of hope. It acknowledges the emotional and legal challenges faced by non-offending parents striving to safeguard their children. While the review is in its early stages, it signifies a proactive step towards aligning Scotland's family law with the paramount principle of child welfare.
