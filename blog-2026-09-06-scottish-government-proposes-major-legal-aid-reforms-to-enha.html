Scottish Government Proposes Major Legal Aid Reforms to Enhance Access to Justice

6 September 2026

The Scottish Government has recently launched a consultation on a new Legal Aid (Scotland) Bill, aiming to modernise the legal aid system and enhance access to justice for all citizens. This initiative seeks to create a simpler, more sustainable, and user-focused framework for publicly funded legal assistance.

Legal aid is a vital service that provides financial assistance to individuals who cannot afford legal representation. It ensures that everyone, regardless of their financial situation, has the opportunity to pursue or defend their legal rights. The current system, governed by the Legal Aid (Scotland) Act 1986, has been in place for several decades and is now under review to better meet the needs of today's society.

The proposed reforms include simplifying processes to reduce duplication and support quicker decision-making. This means that individuals seeking legal aid could experience a more streamlined application process, leading to faster access to necessary legal services. Additionally, the reforms aim to improve access for those who use legal aid and provide greater autonomy for accredited solicitors, allowing them to deliver services more effectively.

One significant aspect of the proposed changes is the introduction of clearer and fairer rules regarding financial contributions from those who can afford to pay towards their legal aid. The current system has been criticised for being confusing and, at times, unfair, with individuals on similar incomes required to pay different amounts. The new plan suggests a more transparent approach, ensuring that payment amounts are easier to understand and equitable.

These reforms are part of a broader effort to build a legal aid system that works better for both the people who rely on it and the professionals who deliver it. The Scottish Government is committed to creating a system that is fairer, more accessible, and fit for the future.

The consultation is open until 13 October 2026, and the government encourages all interested parties to share their views. This is an important opportunity for individuals and organisations to contribute to shaping the future of legal aid in Scotland.
