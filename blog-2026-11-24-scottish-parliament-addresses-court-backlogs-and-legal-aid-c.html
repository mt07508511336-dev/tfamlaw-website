Scottish Parliament Addresses Court Backlogs and Legal Aid Challenges

24 November 2026

Recent developments in Scotland's justice system have brought to light significant challenges affecting court efficiency and access to legal aid. A report published by the Scottish Parliament's Criminal Justice Committee on 19 November 2026 underscores these pressing issues and recommends actionable reforms.

One of the primary concerns highlighted is the persistent backlog in court cases, particularly in the High Court. Victims, witnesses, and accused individuals are experiencing prolonged delays, with trial scheduling extending well into future years. This situation not only prolongs uncertainty but also places immense strain on all parties involved. The report notes that such delays can lead to "significant secondary trauma for victims and witnesses, often leading to witness attrition."

To address these challenges, the Committee recommends that the Scottish Government provide sufficient core revenue funding for the Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service (COPFS) and the Scottish Courts and Tribunals Service (SCTS). This funding is essential to manage increasing solemn business, rising High Court petition volumes, and existing case backlogs without further undermining workforce resilience. The Committee emphasizes that funding decisions should be informed by a comprehensive assessment of the staffing and professional resources required across the prosecution service, courts, defense profession, and advocacy services.

Another critical issue is the contraction of the criminal defense bar, attributed to decades of real-terms reductions in legal aid rates. Since 2005, these rates have fallen by approximately 37% in real terms, making criminal defense work increasingly unsustainable for many solicitors and advocates. This decline poses a structural risk to access to justice, as fewer legal professionals are available to represent individuals in need.

In response, the Committee recommends that the Scottish Government provide sustained operational funding to SCTS and COPFS to maintain and embed Summary Case Management principles across all Sheriff Courts. This approach aims to reduce trial adjournments, court backlogs, and unnecessary witness attendances. Additionally, the Committee suggests establishing measurable targets for reducing adjournments, court backlogs, police witness waiting times, and the time taken to conclude criminal proceedings, with progress reported regularly to Parliament.

At Thompson Family Law, we recognize the profound impact these systemic issues have on individuals seeking justice. Delays in court proceedings and challenges in accessing legal representation can exacerbate the stress and uncertainty faced by our clients. We support the Committee's recommendations and advocate for prompt implementation to enhance the efficiency and fairness of Scotland's justice system.
