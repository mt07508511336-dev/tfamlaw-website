Government Proposes Reforms to Protect Cohabiting Couples' Rights

23 November 2026

In recent years, the way people form families in the UK has evolved significantly. More couples are choosing to live together without marrying or entering into civil partnerships. Despite this shift, the legal system has not kept pace, leaving many cohabiting couples without essential rights when their relationships end.

Recognizing this gap, the UK government has initiated a consultation titled "A Fairer End to Relationships." This initiative seeks to reform family law to better protect cohabiting couples upon separation or death. The consultation focuses on three key areas:

  • Financial Remedies on Divorce and Dissolution: Evaluating how financial matters are settled when marriages or civil partnerships end.
  • Protections for Cohabitants on Separation: Considering legal safeguards for couples who live together but aren't married or in a civil partnership.
  • Inheritance Provision for Cohabitants on Death: Addressing the rights of surviving partners in cohabiting relationships.

Currently, cohabiting couples have limited legal protections compared to their married counterparts. This disparity can lead to financial hardship and uncertainty, especially when relationships dissolve unexpectedly. The government's consultation aims to modernize the law, ensuring it reflects the realities of contemporary family structures and provides fair outcomes for all involved.
