Scottish Government Proposes Legal Aid Reforms to Enhance Access to Justice

4 October 2026

The Scottish Government has recently initiated a consultation on a new Legal Aid (Scotland) Bill, aiming to modernize the current system and make it more accessible and user-friendly. This move is part of a broader effort to ensure that legal assistance is available to those who need it most, without unnecessary complexity or barriers.

Legal aid is a crucial service that provides financial assistance to individuals who cannot afford legal representation. It covers various areas, including family law, civil disputes, and criminal cases. However, the existing system has been criticized for its complexity and inefficiency, leading to challenges for both solicitors and clients.

One of the key proposals in the consultation is to simplify the processes involved in applying for and receiving legal aid. The current system is often seen as cumbersome, with multiple types of legal aid applying in different circumstances. The proposed reforms aim to create a more coherent and straightforward framework, reducing administrative burdens and making it easier for individuals to access the support they need.

Another significant aspect of the proposed reforms is the introduction of clearer and fairer rules regarding financial contributions from those receiving legal aid. The current system can be confusing, with individuals on similar incomes sometimes required to pay different amounts. The new plan suggests a more transparent approach, ensuring that contributions are consistent and based on clear criteria.

These reforms are particularly timely, given the ongoing challenges in the legal aid sector. Recent reports have highlighted a decline in the number of solicitors offering legal aid services, leading to so-called "legal aid deserts" in certain areas. This shortage has made it increasingly difficult for individuals to find legal representation, especially in rural regions.

At Thompson Family Law, we welcome these proposed changes. As a family-run firm with offices in Glasgow, Edinburgh, Coatbridge, and Airdrie, we understand the importance of accessible legal services. Simplifying the legal aid system and ensuring fair compensation for solicitors will help us continue to provide high-quality support to our clients.

The consultation is open until 13 October 2026, and the Scottish Government is encouraging feedback from the public and legal professionals. We believe that these reforms have the potential to significantly improve access to justice in Scotland, and we look forward to seeing the positive impact they will have on our communities.
