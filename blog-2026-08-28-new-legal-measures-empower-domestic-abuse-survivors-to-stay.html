New Legal Measures Empower Domestic Abuse Survivors to Stay in Their Homes

28 August 2026

As of 1 August 2026, significant legal changes have come into effect in Scotland, offering enhanced protection for survivors of domestic abuse. These reforms empower social landlords to take decisive action, ensuring that victims can remain safely in their homes while perpetrators are removed.

Under the new provisions of the Domestic Abuse (Protection) (Scotland) Act 2021, social landlords now have the authority to apply to the court to end the tenancy of an abuser. This means that, rather than the victim being forced to leave their home to escape abuse, the perpetrator can be legally removed from the tenancy agreement. Consequently, the victim can continue living in their home, maintaining stability for themselves and any children involved.

These changes are designed to place accountability squarely on the abuser, rather than the victim. By allowing the tenancy to be transferred to the victim, the law aims to prevent homelessness among survivors and enable children to remain in their familiar environment, including their school and community, where it is safe to do so.

It's important to note that these measures apply to all Scottish Secure Tenancy (SST) tenants, including those whose tenancies began before 1 August 2026. The new legal framework provides a clear mechanism for social landlords to support victims effectively, ensuring their safety and well-being.
