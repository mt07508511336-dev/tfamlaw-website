Legal Aid Shortages Threaten Access to Justice in Scotland

28 September 2026

Recent reports have brought to light a concerning shortage of legal aid providers across Scotland, raising alarms about the accessibility of justice for those who need it most. This issue is not just a statistic; it represents real people facing significant legal challenges without the necessary support.

According to a study by the Institute for Fiscal Studies, as of June 2026, approximately 3.5 million people in England and Wales reside in areas without any active civil legal aid providers. While this study focuses on England and Wales, the implications are equally relevant to Scotland, where similar patterns of diminishing legal aid availability have been observed. The reduction in providers means that individuals dealing with issues such as housing disputes, family breakdowns, or welfare concerns may find it increasingly difficult to obtain the legal assistance they are entitled to.

The consequences of this shortage are profound. Without adequate legal representation, individuals may face unjust outcomes in court, leading to wrongful evictions, loss of custody, or denial of rightful benefits. The Law Society has expressed concern that the lack of legal aid and the rise in self-representation are leading to a high risk of miscarriages of justice. They emphasize that legal aid is a crucial public service that ensures fairness and protects fundamental rights within the justice system.

In response to these challenges, the Legal Aid Agency has been actively seeking to increase capacity in family legal aid services to strengthen access and availability. They have invited organizations to submit bids to deliver family legal aid work in various areas, aiming to support timely access to legal advice and representation. This initiative underscores the urgent need to bolster the legal aid infrastructure to meet the growing demand.
