Scottish Government Releases Analysis of Family Law Reform Consultation

18 November 2026

The Scottish Government has recently published an analysis of responses to its public consultation on proposed family law reforms. This consultation, which ran from December 2025 to April 2026, sought public opinion on several key areas, including the financial rights of cohabitants, the minimum age for marriage, and the simplification of divorce procedures involving children under 16.

One of the primary focuses was on improving the financial rights of cohabitants. Currently, cohabitants in Scotland have limited financial claims compared to married couples or civil partners. The consultation revealed a divided public opinion: 49% agreed with the proposed changes to financial arrangements when cohabitation ends, while 39% disagreed, and 12% were uncertain. This indicates a need for careful consideration to balance the rights and responsibilities of cohabiting partners.

Another significant topic was the proposal to raise the minimum age of marriage to 18. This aligns with international trends aiming to protect young individuals from early marriage and its associated challenges. The consultation also explored extending simplified divorce procedures to cases involving children under 16, aiming to reduce the emotional and financial strain on families during separation.

These proposed reforms reflect the government's commitment to modernizing family law to better serve Scotland's diverse families.
