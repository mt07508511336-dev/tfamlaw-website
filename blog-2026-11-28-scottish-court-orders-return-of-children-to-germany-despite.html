Scottish Court Orders Return of Children to Germany Despite Objections

28 November 2026

In a recent decision, the Outer House of the Court of Session in Scotland ruled that four children, who had been wrongfully removed from Germany, must be returned there. This case highlights the complexities involved when international child abduction intersects with family law.

The father of the children opposed their return, citing two primary defenses: the children's objections and the potential grave risk of harm upon their return. The court, however, found that the objections of the three older children were significantly influenced by their father, rendering them less credible. Additionally, the court determined that the father failed to provide sufficient evidence to substantiate claims that returning the children would expose them to a grave risk of harm.

This ruling underscores the importance of adhering to international agreements, such as the Hague Convention on the Civil Aspects of International Child Abduction, which aims to protect children from wrongful removal across borders. It also emphasizes the necessity for courts to critically assess the authenticity of children's objections and the validity of claims regarding potential harm.

For families navigating international custody disputes, this case serves as a reminder of the legal principles that prioritize the prompt return of children to their habitual residence, barring compelling evidence to the contrary. It also highlights the need for parents to present clear and convincing evidence when raising defenses against such returns.
