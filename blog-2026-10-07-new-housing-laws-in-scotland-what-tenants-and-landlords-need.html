New Housing Laws in Scotland: What Tenants and Landlords Need to Know

7 October 2026

As of 6 October 2026, significant changes to Scottish housing law have come into force, affecting both tenants and landlords. These reforms aim to enhance tenant protections and clarify landlord responsibilities. Here's a breakdown of the key updates:

Addressing Damp and Mould: Awaab's Law

Named in memory of two-year-old Awaab Ishak, who tragically died due to prolonged exposure to mould, Awaab's Law introduces strict timelines for landlords to tackle damp and mould issues:

  • Investigation: Landlords must investigate reported damp or mould within 10 working days of becoming aware of the issue.
  • Communication: Tenants should receive a written summary of the findings within three working days after the investigation concludes.
  • Repairs: Necessary safety repairs must commence within five working days following the investigation's completion.

These measures ensure that damp and mould problems are addressed promptly, safeguarding tenants' health and well-being. For more details, refer to the Scottish Housing News article on Awaab's Law. https://www.scottishhousingnews.com/articles/housing-secretary-welcomes-awaabs-law-coming-into-force-today

Increased Penalties for Wrongful Termination

Tenants now have stronger recourse if they believe their tenancy was wrongfully terminated. The First-tier Tribunal can award compensation ranging from three to 36 times the monthly rent, a substantial increase from the previous cap of six months' rent. This change underscores the importance of landlords adhering to proper eviction procedures. More information is available on Property118. https://www.property118.com/scottish-landlords-warned-over-new-rules-and-fines-of-up-to-36-months-rent/

Revised Succession Rights

The qualifying period for inheriting a private residential tenancy has been reduced from 12 months to six. This change benefits family members and carers, allowing them to succeed a tenancy after a tenant's death, provided they have lived in the property for at least six months. This adjustment offers greater flexibility and support during challenging times. Details can be found in the Scottish Housing News article. https://www.scottishhousingnews.com/articles/housing-secretary-welcomes-awaabs-law-coming-into-force-today

Implications for Tenants and Landlords

For tenants, these reforms provide enhanced protections and clearer rights, ensuring healthier living conditions and fair treatment. Landlords must familiarize themselves with these new obligations to remain compliant and avoid significant penalties. Staying informed and proactive is essential in navigating these changes.
