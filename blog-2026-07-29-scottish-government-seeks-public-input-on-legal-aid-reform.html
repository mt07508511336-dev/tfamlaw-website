Scottish Government Seeks Public Input on Legal Aid Reform

29 July 2026

The Scottish Government has recently launched a consultation to gather public opinions on proposed reforms to the legal aid system. This initiative aims to modernize the framework, making it more efficient and user-friendly for both clients and legal professionals.

Legal aid is a crucial service that provides financial assistance to individuals who cannot afford legal representation. It ensures that everyone has access to justice, regardless of their financial situation. However, the current system, governed by the Legal Aid (Scotland) Act 1986, has been in place for several decades and is due for an update to better meet the needs of today's society.

The proposed reforms focus on several key areas:

  • Simplifying Processes: Streamlining procedures to reduce duplication and support quicker decision-making, thereby improving access for those who use legal aid services.
  • Enhancing Access: Making it easier for individuals to understand and apply for legal aid, ensuring that more people can benefit from the support available.
  • Empowering Solicitors: Providing greater autonomy for accredited solicitors, allowing them to deliver services more effectively.

These changes are part of a broader effort to create a legal aid system that is fairer, more accessible, and fit for the future. The consultation is open until 13 October 2026, and the government is encouraging all interested parties to share their views.

At Thompson Family Law, we understand the importance of a robust legal aid system in ensuring access to justice for all. We welcome these proposed reforms and encourage our clients and colleagues to participate in the consultation process. Your input can help shape a legal aid system that better serves the needs of our communities.

For more information on the consultation and how to participate, please visit the Scottish Government's official website.
