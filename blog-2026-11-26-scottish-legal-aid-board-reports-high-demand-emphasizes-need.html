Scottish Legal Aid Board Reports High Demand, Emphasizes Need for Reform

26 November 2026

The Scottish Legal Aid Board (SLAB) has recently published its annual report for the financial year ending 31 March 2026, revealing a significant demand for legal aid services across Scotland. The report indicates that there were 165,000 grants of legal assistance during this period, with total expenditures reaching £167 million—the second-highest on record in cash terms. This underscores the critical role legal aid plays in ensuring access to justice for many individuals and families.

Despite the high level of service provided, the report highlights challenges within a system designed over 70 years ago. Notably, the current structure does not guarantee access to a solicitor for everyone in need. This situation has led to 'legal aid deserts' in certain areas, where individuals struggle to find solicitors willing to undertake legal aid work. Factors contributing to this issue include fee levels and administrative complexities, which have been points of contention between the legal profession and the government.

In response to these challenges, SLAB has reiterated its commitment to a comprehensive overhaul of the legal aid system. Proposed reforms aim to modernize processes, simplify procedures, and ensure fair compensation for legal professionals. These changes are intended to create a more efficient and accessible system that better serves both clients and practitioners.
