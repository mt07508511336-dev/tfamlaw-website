Legal Aid Deserts Leave Millions Without Access to Justice

15 September 2026

Recent findings have brought to light a concerning trend: over 14 million individuals across England and Wales now reside in areas devoid of local legal aid services. This phenomenon, often termed 'legal aid deserts,' signifies regions where individuals cannot access the legal support they might urgently require.

Legal aid is a government-funded initiative designed to ensure that those who cannot afford legal representation still have access to justice. It covers various areas, including family disputes, housing issues, and immigration matters. However, the diminishing number of legal aid providers has led to vast regions without adequate coverage.

The implications are profound. Families facing eviction, individuals embroiled in custody battles, or those seeking asylum may find themselves without the necessary legal guidance. This lack of support can lead to prolonged legal battles, unfavorable outcomes, and, in some cases, miscarriages of justice.

Several factors contribute to this decline. Reduced funding for legal aid services has made it financially unviable for many firms to continue offering such support. Additionally, the administrative burden associated with legal aid cases can deter practitioners from taking them on.

At Thompson Family Law, we recognize the critical importance of accessible legal services. While our offices in Glasgow, Edinburgh, Coatbridge, and Airdrie continue to offer comprehensive support in family law, civil disputes, and legal aid cases, we are acutely aware of the broader challenges facing the legal community and the public.

Addressing this crisis requires a multifaceted approach. Increased funding, streamlined administrative processes, and incentives for legal professionals to engage in legal aid work are essential steps. Moreover, raising public awareness about the availability and importance of legal aid can empower individuals to seek assistance when needed.

Access to justice is a cornerstone of a fair society. Ensuring that every individual, regardless of their financial situation, has the means to defend their rights is not just a legal obligation but a moral one. As we navigate these challenging times, it is imperative for both the legal community and policymakers to collaborate in bridging the gaps and restoring comprehensive legal aid coverage across the nation.
