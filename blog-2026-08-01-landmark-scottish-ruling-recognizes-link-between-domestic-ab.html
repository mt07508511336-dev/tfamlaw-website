Landmark Scottish Ruling Recognizes Link Between Domestic Abuse and Suicide

1 August 2026

In a groundbreaking decision, the Scottish High Court has convicted Lee Milne of culpable homicide for the death of his wife, Kimberly, who took her own life in July 2023. This marks the first time in Scotland that a perpetrator of domestic abuse has been held legally responsible for driving a victim to suicide, highlighting the profound impact of emotional and psychological abuse.

Throughout their relationship, Milne subjected Kimberly to persistent violence and controlling behavior. On the day of her death, CCTV footage captured a heated argument between the couple, during which Milne trapped Kimberly against a wall. Witnesses described her as "terrified." Shortly after this incident, Kimberly tragically ended her life by jumping from a motorway bridge in Dundee.

This ruling signifies a pivotal shift in the Scottish justice system's approach to domestic abuse cases. By acknowledging that the repercussions of such abuse can extend to suicide, the court has set a precedent for future cases. Campaigners and support organizations have long advocated for this recognition, emphasizing that emotional and psychological abuse can be as damaging as physical violence.

Laura Buchan, legal director for death investigations in the Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service, stated, "This case demonstrates that emotional and psychological abuse can be as dangerous as physical abuse." This sentiment underscores the necessity for a comprehensive understanding of abuse and its potential outcomes.

For victims and survivors, this ruling offers a glimmer of hope. It sends a clear message that the justice system is evolving to better protect those affected by domestic abuse. However, it also serves as a somber reminder of the devastating consequences that can arise when abuse goes unaddressed.
