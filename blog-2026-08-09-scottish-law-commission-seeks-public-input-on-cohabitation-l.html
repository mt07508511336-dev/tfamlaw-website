Scottish Law Commission Seeks Public Input on Cohabitation Law Reform

9 August 2026

In a significant move to modernize family law, the Scottish Law Commission has released a Discussion Paper inviting public feedback on potential reforms to cohabitation laws. This initiative aims to address the evolving nature of relationships and ensure that the legal framework adequately protects the rights of unmarried couples living together.

Over recent decades, Scotland has witnessed a steady increase in the number of couples choosing to cohabit without formalizing their relationships through marriage or civil partnerships. Despite this societal shift, the legal protections for cohabiting partners have remained relatively static, leading to concerns about their adequacy in safeguarding individuals' rights upon relationship breakdown.

The current legislation, primarily governed by the Family Law (Scotland) Act 2006, provides certain rights to cohabitants. However, these provisions have been criticized for being outdated, ambiguous, and overly complex. Key areas of concern include the definition of a "cohabitant," the criteria for financial provision upon separation, the time limits for making claims, and the limited remedies available.

Recognizing these issues, the Scottish Law Commission's Discussion Paper seeks to explore several critical questions:

  • Should the legal framework for cohabitants upon relationship breakdown align more closely with that of married couples and civil partners?
  • How should "cohabitant" be defined to reflect the diversity of modern relationships?
  • Is there a need to establish a minimum duration of cohabitation before individuals can access financial remedies?

By addressing these questions, the Commission aims to develop a more coherent and equitable legal approach that reflects contemporary societal norms and provides clearer guidance for cohabiting couples.

At Thompson Family Law, we understand the complexities and emotional challenges that can arise when cohabiting relationships end. The lack of clear legal protections can lead to uncertainty and hardship, particularly when it comes to financial matters and property rights. We commend the Scottish Law Commission's proactive approach in seeking to reform this area of law and encourage individuals to participate in the consultation process.

Engaging with this consultation offers a valuable opportunity for the public to share their experiences and perspectives, ensuring that any legislative changes are informed by the realities faced by cohabiting couples. Your input can help shape a legal framework that is fair, transparent, and responsive to the needs of all individuals, regardless of their marital status.

For those currently in cohabiting relationships, we recommend taking proactive steps to protect your interests. This can include drafting a cohabitation agreement that outlines financial arrangements and property rights, providing clarity and security for both parties. Our team at Thompson Family Law is here to offer guidance and support in navigating these matters, ensuring that your rights are safeguarded.

As the landscape of family relationships continues to evolve, it is imperative that our legal systems adapt accordingly. The Scottish Law Commission's review of cohabitation laws represents a crucial step toward achieving a more inclusive and equitable legal framework for all couples in Scotland.
