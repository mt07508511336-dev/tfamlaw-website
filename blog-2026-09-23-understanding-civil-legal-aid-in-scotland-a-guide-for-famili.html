Understanding Civil Legal Aid in Scotland: A Guide for Families

23 September 2026

At Thompson Family Law, we understand that navigating legal issues can be daunting, especially when financial concerns are involved. That's why we want to shed light on civil legal aid in Scotland—a vital resource designed to ensure that everyone has access to justice, regardless of their financial situation.

What is Civil Legal Aid?

Civil legal aid helps cover the costs of legal advice and representation in civil cases. This includes matters such as divorce, child custody disputes, housing issues, and more. The goal is to provide support to those who might otherwise be unable to afford legal assistance.

Who is Eligible?

Eligibility for civil legal aid depends on several factors:

  • Financial Assessment: Your income and savings are evaluated to determine if you can afford legal costs. If you're receiving certain benefits like Universal Credit or Income Support, you may automatically qualify without needing to contribute financially.
  • Merit of the Case: The Scottish Legal Aid Board (SLAB) assesses whether your case has a reasonable chance of success and if it's reasonable to spend public funds on it.

How to Apply

To apply for civil legal aid:

  1. Find a Solicitor: Choose a solicitor who offers legal aid services. They will guide you through the application process.
  2. Provide Financial Information: You'll need to share details about your income, savings, and any benefits you receive.
  3. Application Submission: Your solicitor will submit the application to SLAB on your behalf.

What Costs Are Covered?

Civil legal aid can cover various expenses, including:

  • Solicitor fees
  • Court costs
  • Expert reports

Depending on your financial situation, you might be required to contribute towards these costs. In some cases, you may not have to pay anything at all.

Recent Developments

It's important to note that the landscape of legal aid is continually evolving. For instance, the Scottish Government has been working on modernising family law to better reflect societal changes and improve the family justice system. This includes reforms aimed at strengthening children's rights and protecting victims of domestic abuse. Staying informed about these developments can help you understand how they might impact your case.
